Design Notes: HitterLoop Simplified App

This prototype delivers the complete 7-screen simplified flow described in the SIMPLIFIED_APP_SPEC.md.

Design Decisions

  • Color Tokens: Reused the "infield dirt at night" palette from coach.css (dark slate backgrounds, clay primary accents, warm sand text). This guarantees high contrast (3:1+) for outdoor legibility.
  • Typography: Preserved Oswald (display face) for headers/numbers to maintain the athletic scoreboard vibe, and Inter (sans-serif) for body text to ensure readability at small sizes.
  • Touch Targets: Enforced the minimum 48px touch target constraint (via --touch-min) on all buttons, tabs, and interactive rows. This ensures the app is easily usable one-handed by a parent holding a phone.
  • Thumb-friendly Layouts: Actions are anchored to the bottom (sheets) or full-width buttons. Data entry is minimized to optional chips.
  • Language & Tone: The UI uses plain, encouraging language. "What looked good" is listed before the "One fix", and drills clearly state their "Best for" intent up front so parents can make confident choices without baseball knowledge.
  • Progress Visualization: Replaced complex charts with clear status pills ("Showing up in 2 of her last 5 at-bats") and side-by-side visual comparison, matching the spec's requirement for evidence-based reporting without fake precision.

Spec Clarifications Revealed by the UI

During design, a few minor ambiguities in the spec were resolved:

  • The "Record at-bat" confirm sheet doesn't explicitly mention what happens if you select "Skip" for the result chip. I designed the chips as a toggle group where nothing is pre-selected by default, treating the absence of selection as "Skip".
  • For the "Couldn't read this one" state, the spec says "The relevant filming tip from §6". I added a small tip box directly into the report to avoid making the user tap out to a separate screen just to see why it failed.
