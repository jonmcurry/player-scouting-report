## Contradiction 1: Cueing philosophy — external-focus vs. explicit internal-mechanics cue

**Concept:** game_approach / posture (cueing methodology, cuts across both)

**Claim A:** `DRV1-001` (Driveline Baseball, Video 1) — External focus cues
(ball flight/target outcome) promote superior force production and movement
sequencing compared to internal focus cues (body mechanics). Cites
motor-learning research on screen (Wulf 2010, Wu 2012, Castaneda & Gray
2007, per Gemini's extraction). Companion claim `DRV1-002` extends this into
a general coaching-design principle: manipulate constraints rather than
over-cue mechanics verbally.

**Claim B:** `BLM-001` (Blast Motion, Video 4) — Explicit, direct
internal-body coaching cue: "Rotate around your center so the pipe comes to
the tee — don't dive your chest forward." This is precisely the kind of
internal, body-mechanics-focused cue Claim A's research argues is inferior
to external-focus cueing.

**Sources:** Driveline Baseball vs. Blast Motion

**Semantic vs. substantive conflict:** **Substantive.** This is not a
wording difference — it's a real disagreement about coaching-cue style
between two credible sources, with Claim A backed by cited (if
human-unverified) motor-learning research and Claim B representing
widespread, common coaching practice (explicit body-position cues remain
extremely common across hitting instruction generally, including
elsewhere in this same seed set — see the related-but-not-contradictory
notes below).

**Human decision needed:** Should HitterLoop's own AI-drafted coaching-cue
language lean toward external-focus phrasing (per Driveline's claim) even
when displaying or referencing a claim/drill from a source that itself uses
internal-mechanics cue language (like Blast Motion's PVC pipe drill)? Or
should both cueing styles be allowed to coexist as legitimate, source-
attributed alternatives, with HitterLoop simply presenting each claim's own
original cue rather than imposing one philosophy uniformly? This has real
product implications beyond just these two claims, since it touches how
*any* future coaching-cue text gets phrased.

**Suggested `contradiction_group`:** `cueing-philosophy-internal-vs-external`
**Suggested `contradiction_state`:** `open`

## Related, but NOT flagged as a hard contradiction

Recorded here for transparency, since the task asked to actively compare
claims across all videos — these are genuine overlaps/adjacencies worth a
human's attention, but do not rise to "two sources say incompatible things
about the same proposition."

### "Lunging"/"diving" terminology overlap (Driveline vs. Blast Motion)

`DRV2-001` (Driveline, Video 2) describes "lunging at the ball" as a
downstream *symptom* of early loading (a timing-root-cause framing).
`BLM-001` (Blast Motion, Video 4) describes "diving the chest forward" as a
posture fault corrected by rotating around center (a posture/mechanics-root-
cause framing, not explicitly tied to timing).

These may be describing the same or an overlapping observable pattern from
two different causal angles (timing-driven vs. posture-driven), or they may
be two genuinely distinct faults that happen to look superficially similar
on video. **This is exactly the kind of "possible duplicate, needs human
judgment" case the review workflow anticipates** — a human reviewer should
watch both source clips (or the same HitterLoop swing evidence) side by
side before deciding whether these are the same underlying issue described
two ways, or two different issues.

### Mechanical timing diagnosis vs. mental timing approach

`DRV2-001` (Driveline, Video 2) is a *mechanical* diagnosis of what an early
swing looks like and why (early loading → forward move → hip extension →
lunging). `ABCA-LB-001` (ABCA/Bryson LeBlanc, Video 9) is a *mental/approach*
claim about having a timing anchor belief ("be on time for his best
fastball"). These are not contradictory — they operate in different domains
(observable mechanics vs. mental framework) — but a human reviewer should be
aware both exist under the same broad "timing" concept label, which may be
worth splitting once/if an approach-knowledge domain is formally added (see
`methodology-seed-v1.md`'s Video 9 human review notes).

### General kinetic-chain sequencing vs. constraint-based coaching design

`SCI-002` (Let's Have This Conversation/Chad Miller, Video 10) describes
proximal-to-distal kinetic-chain sequencing (ground → hips → shoulders →
hands → barrel). `DRV1-002` (Driveline, Video 1) describes a
constraint-based approach to letting hitters "self-organize" efficient
mechanics. These are compatible, not contradictory — SCI-002 describes *what*
efficient sequencing looks like, DRV1-002 describes *how* to coach toward it
without over-cueing. Noted for completeness, not as a conflict.
